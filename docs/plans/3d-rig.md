# 3D rig for exercise demonstrations

**Date:** 2026-11-23  
**Status:** All 189 guides have a paired local demonstration. Clinical and physical-device release gates remain open.

Use the [motion studio guide](../reference/motion-studio.md) to author a movement or export a matched pair from the current implementation. The requirements below describe the full release target.

Anatomy Explorer should show how a movement is performed and which structures it targets. Use the app’s existing anatomy body and its articulated rig to play authored movements with explicit joint and contact constraints. A clothed human view should demonstrate the same movement with the same side, timing and camera angle. Both views should be repeatable on a laptop or phone.

The failed LTX push-up gives us a concrete acceptance test. With the motion running, the palms and toes stay where they were placed, the skeleton keeps its shape, and the exercise reaches and returns from the intended position. The rig controls those properties. Muscle and nerve colours come from the guide’s target IDs and follow the corresponding meshes.

## Starting point and scope

The [current rig](../../src/motion/movementRig.ts) poses the exact source-derived anatomy used in Explore. It plays a backbend, moonwalk and cartwheel. It binds skeletal surfaces to individual joints and uses blended weights for other tissue. The [current movement checks](../../checks/check-pose.ts) exercise selected poses and synthetic mesh fixtures. Exercise playback needs validation across the full clip and on the actual anatomy assets at each supported level of detail.

The [guide library](../reference/exercise-guides.md) has 163 stretches and 26 nerve glides. A guide card already shows the exercise steps, targets, dose and source, and can be opened from a shareable link. All 189 cards now link to a movement. The pilot acceptance fixtures remain regression tests. The [animation lesson](../animation-and-rigs.md) explains how the underlying rig works.

## Learner experience

1. Opening a guide card shows the existing steps, citations and target highlights. When an eligible demonstration exists, **Show movement** opens the labelled setup pose. **Play** starts one demonstration. **Pause**, **Replay**, phase buttons and a keyboard-operable timeline let the learner inspect it. The default ending is the authored final frame. A repeat option is available only for clips with a verified, continuous repeat.
2. The setup pose begins in the position described by the guide. A lying or seated clip can start on the floor or chair. A transition from standing is included only when it belongs to the authored movement. Show a short phase label alongside the matching written step. Demonstration timing and replay speed are shown separately from the exercise dose.
3. For an exercise with a working side, default to the side named in the guide or the approved default for a symmetric entry. **Left side** and **Right side** always describe the performer. Switching sides resets the demonstration to setup and updates the camera, visible labels and highlighted targets in the same update. Bilateral movements show both sides together. The UI only offers sides with valid motion and matching geometry.
4. **Anatomy** and **Human** use one timeline. Switching views preserves the side, time, phase, playback rate and paused or playing state once the new view is ready. A load pauses the clock at the visible frame until the swap is possible. The anatomy view shows the participating structures and a legend for primary and secondary targets. The human view uses a rights-cleared clothed avatar. Both views make the hands, feet, supports and moving limb visible at the points where they matter.
5. Frame the whole movement and its props when opening the demonstration or pressing **Frame movement**. Keep the camera steady during playback. Orbit and zoom stay available and take precedence over automatic framing. Respect the actual space left by the card on mobile. **Return to anatomy** exits the demonstration and restores the guide’s target view and layer preferences. Changing exercise, closing the card, changing mode or resetting ends the active demonstration and releases its resources. The existing backbend, moonwalk and cartwheel use the Explore controls. Only one movement controls a given rig at a time.
6. The exercise remains accessible as text. Give every control a visible focus state and an accessible name. Phase buttons expose their names and order, the timeline announces time and phase when adjusted, and screen readers receive meaningful state changes rather than frame updates. Make sides and tissues distinguishable by text as well as colour. Reduced motion starts in stills with manual phase navigation, and stops motion and camera transitions if enabled mid-clip. Offer an explicit single-play override and return to still mode afterward. When the card closes, return focus to its originating control when it still exists.

## Motion and anatomy requirements

**One time source.** Bake authored joint poses, validated contacts and phase markers into a versioned motion package. Sample the same timeline in live playback, stills and export. Evaluate a frame from its time and variant so seeking, scrubbing, replay and a different refresh rate produce the same pose at the same point. Bind using the asset pipeline’s registered coordinate system. Store units, joint axes, neutral rotation, left and right conventions, permitted limits and interpolation rules with the rig version. Keep the performer’s left and right independent of camera angle.

**Constrain the motion.** Solve hands, feet and other supports against named contact points using inverse kinematics, which means working back from the required hand or foot position to solve the joints. A contact defines the actor’s landmark and orientation, the target surface or moving body landmark, the active time interval and whether it locks, slides or rolls. Model the mat, chair, wall or doorway as named support surfaces with fixed dimensions per variant. Validate position and orientation at the contact surface, not at a joint centre. A locked palm uses a floor anchor. A hand holding the thigh uses a thigh-relative anchor. An authored release ends the lock before the limb leaves. Changes of support have explicit timing and must pass validation on both sides of the transition.

**Respect reach and clearance.** Check joint limits, segment lengths, elbow and knee direction, and prohibited intersections between the body and props. Use collision proxies tied to the rig and allow only the defined touches and expected adjacent overlaps. Degenerate limb solves must resolve with a consistent bend direction or fail validation. Conflicting contacts, impossible reach and unsupported ranges must produce a location and reason the author can fix. Keep bone transforms rigid and keep the authored sample within its validated interpolation envelope. Validate between keyframes and around support changes. A pose that intersects only between keyframes still fails.

**Use the appropriate movement pattern.** A static stretch shows entry, a hold and release. A dynamic stretch shows the authored direction and return. A slider preserves the guide’s coupled movements and timing. A tensioner or sequence uses its authored order, range and holds. Stepping back through a sequence is a learner control. A seamless loop needs its own verified end-to-start transition. Set the demonstrated range and phase timing per exercise, with the existing guide and review record as the reference.

**Keep the anatomy identifiable.** Bind bone geometry rigidly to its mapped joint with unit scale during the demonstration. Preserve its bind coordinates and catalogue ID. Give soft tissue explicit attachments or checked weights for the range being used, including muscles crossing a joint. Validate against decoded preview, overview and detail assets. Render the highest ready compatible copy at the current pose and keep one visible, pickable copy per structure. The name, highlight, selection and camera bounds follow that copy. Each demo declares which structures must be visible and which posed layers it supports. Unsupported layers remain available in the guide’s target view. A missing required mesh uses the fallback instead of a substitute.

**Treat colour as a target marker.** Use the card’s primary and secondary group membership, resolved to stable structure IDs and the active side. A target keeps its colour across playback and detail changes. Label the highlight as tissue targeted by the guide. A moving nerve overlay illustrates its location and the movement context. Claims about strain, activation, force or recruitment require a separately validated measurement model before they can be shown.

**Match the human view by movement.** Map the timeline’s named joints, contacts and landmarks to the avatar and solve again for its proportions. Both views use identical phase times, side, action order and camera direction. The solve may choose body-specific joint positions to maintain the same support and intent. Validate both views independently. A reach that works for one figure can fail on the other. Keep anatomy picking and target identification in the Anatomy view and preserve the guide card and key phase labels in both views.

## Authoring and publication

Keep the editable pose specifications and reviewer decisions in version control. Start by hand-authoring the three pilot movements. Provide a local author preview with joint values, support and collision overlays, a left/right marker, frame stepping and camera changes. It must show the same baked result as the learner view. A graphical editor or draft generation can follow the pilot if measured authoring and repair time justify it.

| Motion package field   | Requirement                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Identity and revisions | Stable exercise or fixture ID, schema version, rig and solver versions, source and guide revision, asset and prop revisions, and a content fingerprint for the evaluated clip.                                |
| Choreography           | Starting posture, tracks for joints and pelvis, timed phase labels linked to step IDs or step indices under the guide revision, active side and mirror policy, playback duration and supported repeat policy. |
| Contacts and props     | Contact landmarks, support IDs, position and angle tolerances, timing, permitted touches, and dimensions and placement of any prop. Declare all geometry required to show the movement.                       |
| Views and review       | Body and rig mappings, camera presets, target IDs, fallback frames if approved, source and attribution records, validation result, and separate fidelity and clinical review decisions.                       |

Validate the schema before solving and the baked clip before review. Unknown joints or props, duplicate or out-of-order times, non-finite numbers, zero duration, missing phases, incompatible revisions and ambiguous sides must identify the affected clip and field. Reject clips that exceed the declared duration, frame count or package size limits before allocating render resources. Keep the last valid public version available while an edit is a draft, where that version still matches the live guide and has not been withdrawn.

Require a named fidelity reviewer to compare the rendered clip with the written exercise, including side, contacts, holds, prop use and target IDs. For public animated guides, record a named clinical review of the rendered range, sequence and timing alongside the instructions and cautions. Engineering previews can run with a draft status. The viewer’s education-only label stays visible. Bind approval to the guide content, solved motion, rig, solver, visible body and layer mappings, props and published presentation. A change to any of those inputs makes the affected approval stale. Revalidate and review that version before it replaces the current one. Keep drafts and reviewer notes out of public build artifacts.

For authors, export matched, silent 16:9 MP4 clips at a pilot preset of 720p and 30 fps and a still per labelled phase. Use the same sampler and declared camera as the UI. The two views use the same frame count and phase boundaries. Include the side, clip revision, source and attribution with the exported files. Retain the geometry credits in [ATTRIBUTION.md](../../public/models/ATTRIBUTION.md), and record the rights for the human asset and any recorded reference before distributing an output that uses it. Exports run as a local authoring task and should produce a complete set or an actionable error. The rig must play the clip locally from the app’s static assets.

## Edge cases and required behaviour

| Case                                                                                                                                 | Required behaviour                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A joint aims at its own origin, a limb is straight, or the bend direction is ambiguous.                                              | Use the approved bend direction for that pose. Reject a frame if there is no stable valid solution. Show the joint, phase and failed target in the author preview.                                                                            |
| Two hands cannot reach their targets, or a prop change makes a previously reachable pose impossible.                                 | Re-solve against the actual dimensions. Fail the affected variant with the unsatisfied contact and residual. Bone lengths and required contacts remain explicit constraints.                                                                  |
| A foot lifts, pivots or deliberately slides, or a hand changes support.                                                              | Use the declared contact type and release interval. Reject drift during a lock and check the entry, transition and exit for jumps or penetration.                                                                                             |
| Hands touch the thigh, a thigh rests on a seat, or limbs cross in front of each other.                                               | Allow the authored contact pair and check surrounding surfaces. Permit apparent overlap from camera perspective when depth shows separation.                                                                                                  |
| A mirrored move has asymmetric props, source coverage or instructions.                                                               | Mirror positions, signed rotations, contacts, labels and target IDs together. Revalidate the opposite side independently. Keep the authored side only if the mirror diverges from the guide or lacks required data.                           |
| The incoming rig or prop has different axes, scale or handedness.                                                                    | Convert through an explicit versioned mapping before solving. Reject an unknown mapping or one that distorts rigid geometry. Reinspect both sides in the bind and demonstration poses.                                                        |
| A joint needs a movement the 20-joint rig cannot express, or a particular tissue folds, detaches or visibly clips.                   | Use written steps and the existing target view for that variant until the rig or binding supports it. A validated still is available only for a phase that can be shown faithfully on its own.                                                |
| A target is bilateral, midline, grouped or absent on the active side.                                                                | Apply the reviewed side mapping. Keep approved midline targets, identify grouped coverage in the legend and treat an unmapped required target as unavailable.                                                                                 |
| The written step, source or mapped target changes or disagrees with the visual.                                                      | Flag the exact discrepancy for review. Serve the current instructions and target view until a matching visual is approved.                                                                                                                    |
| A paused frame is revisited, a slider is dragged repeatedly, or the playback rate changes.                                           | Re-sample the requested time directly and clamp to the clip range. Preserve phase labels and native control focus. Starting again from the end explicitly replays from setup.                                                                 |
| A paused hold, a step boundary or a repeat seam falls between displayed frames.                                                      | Use the authored boundaries for stepping and seeking. Validate the interpolated contact at the exact boundaries. Disable repeat for an invalid seam and keep Replay available.                                                                |
| Two clips are opened quickly, or the user closes the card or changes side while assets are loading.                                  | The latest valid selection owns the viewport. Discard stale callbacks and prevent any old pose, playback intent or camera request from applying to the new selection.                                                                         |
| The URL uses an old exercise ID, the wrong mode or unknown values.                                                                   | Use the current hash validation and retain valid guide selections. Ignore unknown values and keep the guide usable. Loading or returning to a valid deep link opens its card in a stopped state.                                              |
| The human asset is pending, incompatible or fails to load.                                                                           | Pause at the last visible time. Keep Anatomy usable, show a retry for recoverable failures and hide an unavailable human variant. Switching to the human view after a retry starts at the retained time.                                      |
| The learner hides a required layer.                                                                                                  | Preserve the layer preference and pause with an action to show the required layer for this demonstration. Keep the return to the guide’s target view available.                                                                               |
| A support is off-screen after an orbit, or the viewport rotates.                                                                     | Keep **Frame movement** available. Refit the preset camera to the available space after a resize. Preserve a manually positioned camera until the learner requests a reframe.                                                                 |
| Someone picks a surface while it is moving or remote from its bind position.                                                         | Raycast against the posed, eligible tier. Inspect its stable catalogue ID without changing the active exercise. A guide group change in the picker closes the demonstration if it clears the exercise.                                        |
| Detail loads or is evicted mid-phase, or old detail finishes after the guide changes.                                                | Adopt a compatible replacement at the current sampled time and copy appearance and selection. Continue with the ready tier when refinement fails. Retire stale detail and its associated resources.                                           |
| The tab goes to the background, the browser restores it, or motion preference changes.                                               | Stop scheduling playback and freeze the canonical time. Return paused with the current phase intact. Apply reduced-motion preference immediately and offer stills at the labelled phases.                                                     |
| The browser reports sustained slow frames, memory pressure or page zoom.                                                             | Reduce pixel density or use a validated lower geometry tier. Keep the timeline readout and layout usable. If quality reduction fails to keep playback usable, pause and offer the approved stills with steps.                                 |
| WebGL is unavailable, the context is lost, or allocation fails.                                                                      | Keep the card, sources and instructions usable. Offer matching stills when available. On recovery, rebuild from current state and resume from a paused frame on explicit request.                                                             |
| A pose bundle, Draco asset, prop or image returns 404, stalls, is corrupt, is offline or comes from an incompatible cached revision. | Continue with the compatible tier or approved stills if available. Otherwise show instructions and any usable target view. Time out stalled requests, offer retry for transient errors and log the specific asset and revision for diagnosis. |
| A release omits, withdraws or supersedes a visual while older versions are cached.                                                   | Resolve eligibility from the current manifest and load only matching, content-versioned assets. An unavailable or withdrawn clip falls back to the guide. A rollback restores a complete compatible manifest.                                 |
| A user hits Escape, navigates with the keyboard, uses a screen reader or switches touch orientation.                                 | Close or navigate according to the focused control, return focus predictably, and provide labelled stills and phase navigation without requiring canvas interaction.                                                                          |
| Export is interrupted, disk fills or an encoder fails.                                                                               | Leave prior exports intact. Clean up the incomplete new set, identify the failed phase or operation, and allow the same package to be re-exported.                                                                                            |

## Acceptance and release

The numbers below are proposed engineering tolerances and product targets. The pilot records measured results for the source body and the human variant. Joint ranges come from the reviewed movement, and clinical review is separate from the geometric checks.

| Gate                              | Evidence required                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bone and contact integrity        | In the decoded fixtures, sample the full clip at 60 Hz or denser plus authored events and midpoints around contact changes. Compare runtime samples with the canonical result. Default pilot tolerances are 0.1 mm for bind-space and rigid skeletal distance error, 5 mm for locked contact position error and drift, 5 mm for unapproved penetration on the tested support or collision proxy, and 10° for contact orientation error. Record any stricter per-contact limit in the specification. Review the relevant rendered surfaces from both the teaching and inspection views. |
| Movement and view fidelity        | Every pilot passes its contact and phase checklist and its reviewed side mapping. The two paired movements agree on phase boundaries to within one exported frame. First, middle and last frames, hold and release boundaries, and any repeat seam pass visual review. Watch each full clip alongside its contact sheet.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Validation catches failures       | Run known-bad examples for swapped sides, an unreachable target, a flipped elbow or knee, a hand through the floor, a collision between keys, missing geometry, incorrect phase order and stale review. Each fails with a useful reason. Valid hand-on-thigh and seated contact fixtures pass. `npm run check` remains green and `check:pose` or its successor covers the new fixtures and the existing performances.                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Browser, mobile and accessibility | Exercise real entry points in desktop Chrome on the M4 Pro and Safari on an iPhone. Verify Android Chrome on a named phone before broad release. Record browser, OS and viewport. Cover keyboard-only, reduced motion, one screen reader, touch, 320 px width, the actual device’s portrait and landscape sizes, refresh, deep links, backgrounding, retry and a forced context loss. At least four of five pilot learners can find the demo, identify the working side and replay a phase without help.                                                                               |
| Load and render performance       | Preserve the current 2.5 MB first-paint preview budget in all three modes. Fetch clip, avatar and prop assets after the learner requests the demo. Keep the 3D runtime in the existing lazy scene. On the documented 20 Mbps / 100 ms profile, target a usable first view within 5 s and a setup frame within 2 s of opening the demo once its compatible anatomy previews are ready. Target p95 frame time at or below 33 ms while playing on reference devices. Measure the still fallback when the phone cannot meet the motion target.                                             |
| Resource and state recovery       | Across 20 open/play/switch/close cycles and one forced WebGL recovery, owned rig instances, listeners, timers and GPU buffers return to a stable baseline after settling. The new player requests frames only for visible playback or interaction. Exit returns to the current guide’s target view or the newly selected mode with only its own resources and state.                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Export and credit reproducibility | Export both variants from one motion revision. Compare time stamps, landmarks and framing at named phases with the viewport samples. Check frame count, silent playback and attached attribution. Test interruption and a successful rerun against the same inputs.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |

### Rollout decisions

1. **Prove the rig.** Use the push-up to verify authored floor contacts and inspect actual atlas meshes. Confirm the existing Explore movements still work. If the pilot exposes bad bindings, add the smallest joint or attachment change that the reproduction requires and revalidate the affected motions.
2. **Prove the guide experience.** Add the hamstring stretch and sciatic slider behind a local feature switch. Validate the anatomy and paired human outputs where required, preserve written fallbacks, and record authoring time, review time and device results for each clip. Confirm the human asset and distribution terms before opening that view publicly.
3. **Release the validated subset.** Publish an individual guide demonstration only after its runtime, device, accessibility, fidelity and clinical gates pass. Use the same eligibility rules for an exported clip distributed with the guide. A failing clip can be withdrawn through the manifest and return to its target view without disabling other guides.
4. **Expand after the pilot.** Verify doorway contact and hand articulation before committing to larger batches. Track eligible clips and reasons for text or still fallbacks by exercise type and body area. Use observed authoring and review time on a mixed batch to plan expansion toward all 189 entries.
